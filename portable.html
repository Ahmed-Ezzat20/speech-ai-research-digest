Research profiles

Habibi · TTS — A unified foundation for Arabic dialect TTS

An MSA-first training curriculum, regional conditioning, and released Arabic checkpoints—including Egyptian Arabic.

Build takeaway: Test MSA → Egyptian fine-tuning against unified dialect conditioning on a speaker-disjoint EGY set.

Caveat: Open artifacts are not blanket permission to reuse all source audio. Clear each upstream data license before training, redistribution or commercial use. Paper-reported benchmark results do not establish performance on your own speakers, domains or code-switching data.

F5-TTS · TTS — Flow matching, without an alignment pipeline

A non-autoregressive Diffusion Transformer with open training code. The original base checkpoint is Chinese/English—not an Arabic baseline by default.

Build takeaway: Compare a small Egyptian fine-tune with training from scratch using identical held-out speakers and prompts.

Caveat: Do not infer Arabic capability from the original base checkpoint or Egyptian quality from a community model listing. Document corpus provenance, diacritics, vocabulary and code-switching evaluation separately.

Casablanca · Datasets — Eight dialects. A more honest Arabic ASR test.

Manual transcripts, dialect labels, and code-switching annotations across eight Arabic varieties. Source audio is not redistributed.

Build takeaway: Keep the evaluation slices untouched; build a separately licensed Egyptian code-switching training corpus.

Caveat: This is not a clean, speaker-controlled TTS corpus. Entertainment-video clips may contain multiple speakers, and gender balance varies. Recheck availability and secure lawful audio access; do not repurpose held-out evaluation data for training.

Qwen3-TTS · TTS — An open multi-codebook speech stack to study

Streaming generation with released models and tokenizers. A useful architectural reference, but Arabic is not officially listed.

Build takeaway: Ablate semantic-plus-acoustic codebooks against a simpler tokenizer on consented Egyptian speech.

Caveat: Treat the released stack as an architecture reference, not proof of Arabic quality. Arabic adaptation requires independent data and evaluation. Openness of weights and tokenizers does not imply openness of training audio.

OpenAI Whisper · ASR — A strong baseline is not a dialect benchmark

Public multilingual weights and inference code, with explicit Arabic representation. Egyptian-dialect quality still needs your own evaluation.

Build takeaway: Freeze an Egyptian code-switching test set and let WER/CER error clusters guide your own model priorities.

Caveat: An Arabic token is not evidence of Egyptian accuracy. Use consented, speaker-diverse data and report hallucination, repetition, entity and numeral errors alongside WER/CER. The training corpus is not an open data substitute.

OpenAI gpt-realtime · Competitors — The frontier is moving to direct speech-to-speech

A proprietary realtime audio model rather than a chained ASR–TTS pipeline. Study the capabilities, not an unavailable training recipe.

Build takeaway: Design matched Egyptian tests for turn-taking, code-switching, time-to-first-audio and dialect naturalness.

Caveat: Missing Arabic evidence does not prove failure; it means quality is unverified here. Do not mix separate OpenAI model language claims. Hosted aliases and behavior can change, and internal scores should not be treated as independent benchmarks.

Experiments

Egyptian code-switching ASR · Build an evaluation set before training a better recognizer.

A carefully curated Egyptian Arabic–English corpus could reveal systematic errors that aggregate multilingual scores conceal.

Metrics: Report WER and CER per dialect, code-switch density, noise condition and speaker group. Publish normalization rules and confidence intervals.

  1. Collect consented, speaker-diverse Egyptian Arabic–English speech.
  2. Define transcription rules for loanwords, mixed scripts, punctuation and numerals.
  3. Split by speaker and recording session to prevent leakage.
  4. Establish a frozen baseline, then fine-tune and compare on the same held-out test set.

Deliverable: A versioned evaluation set, dataset card, reproducible baseline and error taxonomy.

Dialect-controllable Arabic TTS · Separate dialect fidelity from voice quality.

Explicit dialect conditioning and consistent text normalization may improve pronunciation without conflating dialect with speaker identity.

Metrics: Track intelligibility, pronunciation errors, speaker similarity, native-listener dialect fidelity, MOS protocol and inference real-time factor. Do not compare MOS across mismatched protocols.

  1. Audit open training code and model/data licenses before selecting a baseline.
  2. Curate consented parallel dialect prompts with pronunciation annotations.
  3. Train a small controlled ablation: baseline versus dialect-conditioned model.
  4. Evaluate unseen speakers and texts with native dialect listeners.

Deliverable: A reproducible dialect-conditioning ablation and an ethically sourced evaluation audio pack.

An Arabic speech data foundry · Make quality and provenance part of the training pipeline.

Transparent provenance, robust deduplication and dialect-aware quality checks could produce a more useful corpus than collecting more unfiltered hours.

Metrics: Track consent coverage, duplicate rate, transcription agreement, clipping/noise, dialect balance and train/test contamination.

  1. Document recording consent, usage rights and retention for every source.
  2. Track audio hashes, source lineage and speaker/session identifiers.
  3. Apply VAD, quality filtering, transcription review and dialect annotations.
  4. Freeze speaker-disjoint splits and publish a dataset card with limitations.

Deliverable: A versioned curation pipeline, schema, quality dashboard and release checklist.
