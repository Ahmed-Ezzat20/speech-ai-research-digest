  1. Beyond Naturalness: Probing Automated Text-To-Speech Evaluators on Linguistically Grounded Dimensions · 2026-10-22T17:31:03Z

    Automated Text-to-Speech (TTS) evaluation methods (Mean Opinion Score (MOS) predictors and Audio Large Language Model (Audio-LLM) judges) are expected to reflect human perception, yet it is unclear how well they capture the distinct aspects of speech that listeners actually perceive. We deconstruct "naturalness" into a linguistically grounded annotation schema spanning 10 distinct perceptual dimensions, and use it to construct the first dimension-level meta-evaluation benchmark for TTS, comprising 860 utterances annotated by trained linguist raters. Results from benchmarking four MOS predictors and four Audio-LLM judges reveal that MOS predictors are more sensitive to acoustic signal-level properties at the prosodic and paralinguistic levels, while Audio-LLM judges show selective, prompt-dependent detection that favors word-level errors and does not generalize across all dimensions. Neither class reliably captures a breadth of linguistically structured, human-perceptible speech errors. Our dataset, annotation schema, and evaluation code are publicly released to support more targeted and interpretable TTS evaluation.

  3. How Far Can the Gemma 4 Speech Encoder Go Without Changing Its Weights? Saudi and Gulf Arabic Speech Recognition with a Frozen and an Adapter-Augmented Encoder · 2026-10-22T16:00:30Z

    We study the 305M-parameter audio encoder shared by Gemma 4 and EmbeddingGemma 2 (bit-identical to that of Gemma 4 E2B except for the final audio-embedding projection) for Saudi and Gulf Arabic speech recognition, without changing any of its pretrained weights; the adapters of our adapted system are additive and kept separate. A frozen system trains only a 22.6M-parameter CTC reader over a learned mix of four intermediate encoder layers and the encoder output; an adapted system adds 3.1M low-rank adapter parameters, a 41.5M-parameter hybrid CTC/attention reader and joint decoding with a sub-word language model.   4. Measuring the Verifier's Floor: How Much Apparent Rare-Word Text-to-Speech Error Is ASR Error? · 2026-10-22T15:59:54Z

    Text-to-speech (TTS) is usually evaluated by transcribing its output with an automatic speech recognition (ASR) system, so the word error rate also counts recognizer errors. We measure these errors on human recordings of the test sentences. On LibriSpeech and FLEURS, the evaluation recognizer misses 33-67% of very rare words even in human speech, and all three recognizers miss 22-42%. Checking every word with two more recognizer families lowers the apparent TTS error on words that at least one recognizer hears two- to three-fold (very rare LibriSpeech words: from 10-13% to 3-5%), yet very rare words remain the hardest. Ignoring words the recognizer misses in human speech is biased: the other recognizers find TTS errors far more often on these words (4-6% vs. 0.2-0.3%) and overturn about half of the errors this mask keeps. As half of the remaining adjudicated errors may be spelling variants, a listening test is needed.

  5. Combining Masked Language Modeling and Cross-Modal Contrastive Learning for Prosody-Aware TTS · 2026-10-22T15:39:28Z

    Text encoders in neural text-to-speech (TTS) see only linguistic content, so prosody must be inferred from paired data and synthesised intonation is often flat. Contrastive text-audio pretraining (CLAPSpeech) injects prosodic knowledge by contrasting acoustic realisations of the same phoneme, but it never trains the encoder to tell different phonemes apart, and its interaction with masked language modelling (MLM) is unexplored. We study a curriculum for a speaker-conditioned phoneme-and-subword encoder: MLM, contrastive alignment over batches of different phonemes, and CLAPSpeech-style same-phoneme refinement. On Russian conversational speech, with each variant conditioning an identical Grad-TTS model, MLM followed by mixed-phoneme alignment gives the lowest word error rate (17.6% versus 32.8% for same-phoneme training alone) and the best quality and intonation ratings. Adding same-phoneme refinement improves same-phoneme retrieval yet yields the worst synthesis: embedding-space retrieval does not predict usefulness for generation.

In this work, we present a comparative analysis of search space complexity and efficiency for Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) and neural transducer models under different pruning strategies and LM configurations.   9. A corpus-only AUC diagnostic for pooled speech-based depression detection · 2026-10-22T13:26:31Z

    When clinical speech corpora are pooled for evaluation, corpus membership can itself become predictive if class prevalences differ across sources. These findings indicate that ASR errors are not random but socially patterned and can be explained from a sociolinguistic perspective. Thus, the study demonstrates the importance of incorporating sociolinguistic expertise into the evaluation and development of speech technologies and argues that more equitable ASR systems require explicit attention to dialectal variation and community-based speech data.
