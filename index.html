Qwen3-TTSArabic not listed

An open multi-codebook speech stack to study

TTS

Streaming generation with released models and tokenizers. A useful architectural reference, but Arabic is not officially listed.

ARCHITECTURE / FORMAT

The 12Hz line uses a 12.5 Hz, 16-layer multi-codebook tokenizer. A semantic first codebook and acoustic residual codebooks feed a dual-track autoregressive LM with multi-token prediction, then a causal ConvNet waveform decoder.

ACTUAL RELEASED ASSETS

Official 0.6B/1.7B 12Hz models, Base voice-cloning/fine-tuning variants, CustomVoice/VoiceDesign variants, and tokenizers. The report describes 5M+ training hours but does not release that corpus or a reusable provenance ledger.

ARABIC & DIALECT EVIDENCE

Official list: Chinese, English, Japanese, Korean, German, French, Russian, Portuguese, Spanish and Italian. Arabic and Egyptian Arabic are not listed; some Chinese dialect voice profiles are documented.

LICENSE & REUSE

Released models and tokenizers are Apache 2.0 according to the technical report; the official 0.6B Base card is Apache-2.0. This does not grant rights to undisclosed training audio.

What the source reports

  • The 12Hz stack combines a 12.5 Hz, 16-layer tokenizer with a dual-track autoregressive LM, multi-token prediction and causal ConvNet decoder.
  • The repository lists 0.6B and 1.7B Base, CustomVoice and VoiceDesign models. Base supports reference-audio cloning and fine-tuning.
  • The ten officially documented languages exclude Arabic and Egyptian dialects.
  • More than five million training hours are described in the report; the underlying corpus is not provided as an open, provenance-documented dataset.
RESEARCH IMPLICATION · OUR PROPOSED EXPERIMENT

Ablate semantic-plus-acoustic codebooks against a simpler tokenizer on consented Egyptian speech.

Before you build: Treat the released stack as an architecture reference, not proof of Arabic quality. Arabic adaptation requires independent data and evaluation. Openness of weights and tokenizers does not imply openness of training audio.

20 Jan 2026Arabic TTS paper
HabibiEgyptian explicit

A unified foundation for Arabic dialect TTS

TTS

An MSA-first training curriculum, regional conditioning, and released Arabic checkpoints—including Egyptian Arabic.

ARCHITECTURE / FORMAT

F5-TTS v1 base using mel spectrograms and raw text characters. Chinese/English initialization → MSA supervised fine-tuning → unified or specialized dialect models, with reference context and optional regional tokens such as EGY.

ACTUAL RELEASED ASSETS

Official training/inference code, checkpoints, evaluation scripts and benchmark artifacts. Raw training data include internally held and public-source recordings; full redistributability is not established.

ARABIC & DIALECT EVIDENCE

Arabic: MSA plus SAU, UAE, ALG, IRQ, EGY, MAR, OMN, TUN, LEV, SDN and LBY regional identifiers. Egyptian Arabic is explicit; the paper notes Saidi/Upper Egyptian in its convention.

LICENSE & REUSE

Code: MIT. Unified, SAU and UAE checkpoints: CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0. Specialized ALG, EGY, IRQ, MAR and MSA checkpoints: Apache 2.0. Upstream dataset rights must be audited independently.

What the source reports

  • The standardized benchmark covers MSA, SAU, UAE, ALG, IRQ, EGY and MAR, with more than 11,000 utterances and manually verified/filtered transcripts.
  • The paper reports 1,857.3 hours and 879,339 utterances after corpus expansion. Its pipeline repurposes ASR data using speaking-rate filtering, denoising, corpus-specific text rules and short-clip merging.
  • The curriculum is MSA first, then dialectal data; reported ablations examine regional identifiers and reference context.
  • Evaluation includes general and dialect-specific ASR measurements, exposing potential evaluator bias across dialects.
RESEARCH IMPLICATION · OUR PROPOSED EXPERIMENT

Test MSA → Egyptian fine-tuning against unified dialect conditioning on a speaker-disjoint EGY set.

Before you build: Open artifacts are not blanket permission to reuse all source audio. Clear each upstream data license before training, redistribution or commercial use. Paper-reported benchmark results do not establish performance on your own speakers, domains or code-switching data.

The frontier is moving to direct speech-to-speech

FRONTIER

A proprietary realtime audio model rather than a chained ASR–TTS pipeline. Study the capabilities, not an unavailable training recipe.

ARCHITECTURE / FORMAT

OpenAI describes a single direct speech-to-speech model, not separate STT and TTS stages. Public interfaces include WebRTC, WebSocket and SIP. The reviewed docs list a 32,000-token context and no fine-tuning support.

ACTUAL RELEASED ASSETS

Paid hosted API. General availability announced 28 Aug 2025. No released training code, model weights or training corpus in the cited sources.

ARABIC & DIALECT EVIDENCE

Announcement examples include Spanish, Chinese, Japanese and French. The reviewed gpt-realtime sources do not explicitly establish Arabic or Egyptian quality. Arabic in a separate GPT-4o mini TTS guide is not evidence for this model.

LICENSE & REUSE

Proprietary OpenAI hosted service governed by service terms and usage policies. No open weights or dataset license is provided in the reviewed sources.

What the source reports

  • OpenAI announced gpt-realtime and general availability of the Realtime API on 28 August 2025.
  • The announcement describes direct audio processing/generation in one model instead of a traditional STT → language model → TTS chain.
  • Customer collaboration is mentioned, but training datasets, weights and independent training recipes are not released.
  • Arabic/Egyptian quality is not established by the reviewed sources; internal provider evaluations are not a reproducible dialect benchmark.
RESEARCH IMPLICATION · OUR PROPOSED EXPERIMENT

Design matched Egyptian tests for turn-taking, code-switching, time-to-first-audio and dialect naturalness.

Before you build: Missing Arabic evidence does not prove failure; it means quality is unverified here. Do not mix separate OpenAI model language claims. Hosted aliases and behavior can change, and internal scores should not be treated as independent benchmarks.

09 Oct 2024Paper first posted
F5-TTSBase: no Arabic claim

Flow matching, without an alignment pipeline

TTS

A non-autoregressive Diffusion Transformer with open training code. The original base checkpoint is Chinese/English—not an Arabic baseline by default.

ARCHITECTURE / FORMAT

Non-autoregressive flow-matching TTS with a Diffusion Transformer and ConvNeXt-based text refinement. The described design needs no separate duration model, text encoder or phoneme alignment. Sway Sampling selects inference flow steps.

ACTUAL RELEASED ASSETS

Open training and fine-tuning entry points, inference code and public checkpoints. The paper describes public multilingual training data; the base model card identifies Emilia Chinese/English data. Community Arabic artifacts are separate releases.

ARABIC & DIALECT EVIDENCE

Original base checkpoints: Chinese and English from Emilia. Arabic/English appears separately in the official community model index through SILMA AI. Reviewed sources do not establish Egyptian-dialect quality.

LICENSE & REUSE

Repository code: MIT. Repository pretrained models: CC-BY-NC because of Emilia data. The separately listed SILMA Arabic/English model is labelled Apache-2.0; check the exact checkpoint and dataset terms.

What the source reports

  • F5-TTS is fully non-autoregressive and uses flow matching with a Diffusion Transformer.
  • ConvNeXt text refinement supports text/speech alignment; Sway Sampling is an inference-time method, not a required retraining step.
  • Training and fine-tuning entry points are released, while the original base model card describes Emilia Chinese/English data.
  • A community SILMA AI Arabic/English model appears in the official index; this listing is not a verified Egyptian-dialect benchmark.
RESEARCH IMPLICATION · OUR PROPOSED EXPERIMENT

Compare a small Egyptian fine-tune with training from scratch using identical held-out speakers and prompts.

Before you build: Do not infer Arabic capability from the original base checkpoint or Egyptian quality from a community model listing. Document corpus provenance, diacritics, vocabulary and code-switching evaluation separately.

Casablanca8 Arabic dialects

Eight dialects. A more honest Arabic ASR test.

DATA

Manual transcripts, dialect labels, and code-switching annotations across eight Arabic varieties. Source audio is not redistributed.

ARCHITECTURE / FORMAT

Arabic dialect dataset and ASR benchmark, not a speech-generation model. The paper evaluates Whisper, SeamlessM4T and MMS, with dialect-specific fine-tuning case studies.

ACTUAL RELEASED ASSETS

The paper reports URLs, timestamps, annotations and metadata, not bundled source recordings. The reviewed Hugging Face card says only validation and test sets are released. Copyright restrictions apply to source audio.

ARABIC & DIALECT EVIDENCE

Algerian, Egyptian, Emirati, Jordanian, Mauritanian/Hassaniya, Moroccan, Palestinian and Yemeni Arabic. Dialect labels are explicit at country level.

LICENSE & REUSE

No permissive dataset license is established by the reviewed card. The paper describes research-only release conditions and original video copyright remaining with owners. Do not assume audio redistribution or commercial training rights.

What the source reports

  • The paper reports 47:53:20 total speech and 40,793 manually annotated segments across eight dialects.
  • Reported Egyptian coverage is approximately 7:04:16 and Moroccan coverage 6:15:02.
  • Annotations include transcription, dialect/country, gender and code-switching. Some Algerian/Moroccan samples include French; other varieties may include English.
  • The current reviewed dataset card releases validation/test material only. The paper withholds source audio because of copyright.
RESEARCH IMPLICATION · OUR PROPOSED EXPERIMENT

Keep the evaluation slices untouched; build a separately licensed Egyptian code-switching training corpus.

Before you build: This is not a clean, speaker-controlled TTS corpus. Entertainment-video clips may contain multiple speakers, and gender balance varies. Recheck availability and secure lawful audio access; do not repurpose held-out evaluation data for training.

OpenAI WhisperArabic · evaluate dialects

A strong baseline is not a dialect benchmark

ASR

Public multilingual weights and inference code, with explicit Arabic representation. Egyptian-dialect quality still needs your own evaluation.

ARCHITECTURE / FORMAT

Encoder–decoder Transformer over log-Mel audio with task/language tokens and a 30-second receptive field. The original design uses 80 Mel channels; large-v3 changes the input to 128 Mel bins.

ACTUAL RELEASED ASSETS

Public inference code and pretrained English/multilingual checkpoints. The original 680,000-hour training corpus is described, not released. The large-v3 card documents a later 1M-hour weakly labelled plus 4M-hour pseudo-labelled mixture.

ARABIC & DIALECT EVIDENCE

Arabic is explicitly represented by the ar/arabic tokenizer entry. Multilingual language support is not a guarantee of Egyptian-dialect quality; model-card caveats note uneven performance by language, accent and dialect.

LICENSE & REUSE

Official code and model weights: MIT. This does not relicense the web-source audio/transcripts or resolve their provenance and downstream usage rights.

What the source reports

  • Special tokens select transcription, translation, language identification and related tasks in a shared sequence-to-sequence framework.
  • Original training uses filtered web audio/transcript pairs segmented into 30-second clips.
  • The paper relates performance to per-language training volume and notes English-heavy collection; multilingual quality is uneven.
  • The model card warns about hallucinated text, repetition and language/accent/dialect disparities.
RESEARCH IMPLICATION · OUR PROPOSED EXPERIMENT

Freeze an Egyptian code-switching test set and let WER/CER error clusters guide your own model priorities.

Before you build: An Arabic token is not evidence of Egyptian accuracy. Use consented, speaker-diverse data and report hallucination, repetition, entity and numeral errors alongside WER/CER. The training corpus is not an open data substitute.

OUR HYPOTHESIS · NOT A REPORTED RESULT

EGYPTIAN ARABIC × ENGLISH · ASR

Build the test set

Make code-switching errors visible. Start with consented, speaker-diverse recordings and a frozen baseline—not a bigger training run.

Open the experiment brief

The hypothesis

A carefully curated Egyptian Arabic–English corpus could reveal systematic errors that aggregate multilingual scores conceal.

A controlled starting point

  1. Collect consented Egyptian Arabic–English recordings with diverse speakers and environments.
  2. Define transcription conventions for loanwords, mixed scripts, numerals and punctuation.
  3. Split by speaker and recording session; freeze the evaluation set before fine-tuning.
  4. Run a fixed checkpoint, cluster its errors, then fine-tune and compare on the same held-out data.

Evaluate honestly

Report WER and CER by code-switch density, noise condition and speaker group. Publish normalization rules, uncertainty intervals and hallucination/entity-error slices. Keep model identity and decoding settings fixed.
